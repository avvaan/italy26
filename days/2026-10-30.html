Rome as a Quest

  • Lisbon
  • Fiumicino
  • Rome
Scroll

A night on the plane, a connection in Lisbon — and in the evening we're in Rome. Fiumicino airport is full of people, as if the whole world had decided to fly into Rome on the same evening.

First of all we rushed to find where to pick up the rental car. That turned out not to be simple. It's somewhere in some Terminal 3 where nothing is signposted. In the end we figured it out: you have to go out of the first exit, cross the road, stand and wait — they'll come for you. We stand, we wait. They came. The first small victory: we have the car. Now we have wheels in a country where, as will turn out later, wheels are not always an advantage.

And at that very moment Masha calls from Lisbon. She went to the wrong terminal, and there are about ten minutes until departure. Remember I said yesterday that she's a grown-up and will manage? Well, here she is, managing. We tell her: skip the line, go up to people, ask, they'll let you through. Ten minutes, a different terminal, a foreign country — anyone would get flustered. And on top of that she's embarrassed. How can you — skip the line, past everyone? We barely talked her into it. And she made it. Afterwards you think: shame is a good feeling, but sometimes it misses the plane along with you.

Next, the hotel. Which nowhere says it's a hotel. No sign, no plaque. No parking, of course. I drove into some courtyard, and it was impossible to drive out of it forwards. I reverse out, by some miracle not touching the tiny Italian cars, which are parked as if someone had placed them by hand. It worked out, not a single scratch. My guardian angel, I think, worked overtime that evening.

Then the real game begins. On the door — someone else's login and password; you enter them and hope. The keys are in the mailbox. Then the stairs and the third floor, which in Italian is not the third at all. Their ground floor is called piano terra, the “earth floor,” and doesn't count. So their third is our fourth, and you climb one more flight with a suitcase than you'd counted on.

Our room is number twelve. We walk down the corridor, and the numbers on the doors start at thirteen. There is no twelve. We found one door with no number at all. That was twelve. Why it's like that — don't ask. Probably so that life wouldn't seem too sweet from the very first evening.

In short, friends, if you've never played quest games, you've got no business in Italy. Although, to be honest, it all ended well: we have the car, we have the keys, the room was found. What more does a person need after a night on a plane?

In these days I started keeping travel notes. I called them “Roman Holiday,” like the old film with Audrey Hepburn. Though for now it's not a holiday for us, more of a survival course for beginners. But everything is still ahead.

We're not taking the car into the center of Rome — yesterday I explained about the ZTL zones. Let it stand by the hotel, and we'll walk. Although tomorrow morning parking at the hotel will have its own story too.

And now we're driving back to the airport to meet Masha. At night. So far we've seen Rome only from the car window, and mostly in reverse at that.

Tomorrow — the Vatican.
