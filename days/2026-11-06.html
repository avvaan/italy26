By Old Paths to Amalfi

  • Agerola
  • Amalfi

9 photos

Scroll

In the morning, dawn over the Gulf of Salerno. You don't see that every day, so we decided to take a fifteen-minute walk. The fifteen-minute walk turned into a descent along old paths all the way to Amalfi. Friends, if on the Amalfi Coast someone suggests you “walk for fifteen minutes,” clarify right away: down or up, and how far.

We set out from San Lazzaro — that's one of the villages of Agerola, high above the sea. Then Tovere, the fork where paths 361 and 317 meet (the paths here have numbers, like in real mountains), then Vettica. These paths are old, truly old. The road along the coast was built only in the nineteenth century, and before that people walked for centuries between the mountain villages and the sea just like this, on foot, over stones and steps. We went the same way, only without a mule and without a load.

By noon we were standing at the cathedral in Amalfi. Masha, a wise girl, stayed home. The girl is twenty-one, but for her dad, as is well known, that changes nothing.

Amalfi today is a small resort town squeezed between cliffs. But once it was a maritime republic, one of the first in Italy, and its merchants traded a great deal with Byzantium. So the ties with Constantinople here are old ones, and that will come in handy in our story.

The Cathedral of Amalfi is several churches at once. The Cloister of Paradise with its arches — it was built in the thirteenth century as a cemetery for the noble families of Amalfi, hence the name. The Basilica of the Crucifix — the old cathedral, which is now a museum. Kept there is an Angevin miter, all embroidered with pearls. And below is the crypt, where the relics of the Apostle Andrew the First-Called lie.

My heavenly patron.

The relics were brought here from Constantinople in 1208, after the crusaders sacked the city. A story, I'll say honestly, that isn't easy for an Orthodox heart. But the apostle doesn't stop being an apostle because of that. Andrew is the first whom Christ called. And the first thing he did was find his brother Simon and say to him: “We have found the Messias.” And he brought him to Christ. That's how Simon became Peter. It seems to me this is all of Andrew: to find Christ and at once bring another to Him. According to tradition, he preached in our lands too and blessed the hills on which Kyiv later rose. He suffered a martyr's death in Greek Patras, on a diagonal cross. And here in Amalfi, according to tradition, an oily moisture called manna seeps from his tomb.

You stand at the relics of a saint you've been reading about in the Gospel all your life, whose name you bear — and you think: when will you ever be this close again.

We have a separate story about the apostle; you can listen to it below.

We went back up by SITA bus. From November 2 they're on the winter timetable, so if you go in the off-season, check in advance when the last bus is. Our legs said thank you.

So that's how a fifteen-minute walk led me to my saint. The most important thing, as often happens, happened not according to plan.
