Thursday

Departure: Orlando — Lisbon

Scroll

Friends, let me start with the main thing: we have taken off. In the evening, from Orlando, without any incidents. For the start of a journey, that is already quite something.

We left home around six in the evening; the plane was at 22:40. We are flying via Lisbon, with a connection there, and then on to Rome, where we are scheduled to land tomorrow at 18:45. The whole trip is a little over two weeks; we return home on November 14, also via Lisbon.

There are three of us: me, Yana and Masha. Masha, though, is on her own flight. We bought the tickets at different times, so in Lisbon our paths will briefly part. Masha is twenty-one, a grown-up, she'll manage. Why am I stressing this, you ask? You'll find out tomorrow.

The luggage was a little lesson too. At first we were sure we were each checking a 23-kilogram suitcase. On the day of departure we reread the conditions once more: checked baggage is included only on my ticket, Yana has none. So between the two of us one suitcase goes in the hold, and the rest is on our shoulders. The moral is simple: read your ticket conditions before you start packing your suitcases, not after.

We rented a car in advance; we'll pick it up right at Fiumicino airport. We took out separate insurance, just in case. We also looked into whether an international driving permit is needed in Italy. It is. We're not planning to drive around Rome, though: the car will stay by the hotel. You can't take it into the center — there are ZTL zones there, which only locals with a permit may enter. Drive in out of ignorance, and half a year later a letter arrives at your home with a fine and a pretty Italian stamp.

We thought about accommodation for a long time. We even wrote to a pilgrim house at a monastery right in the center, near Navona. In the end, for the first nights we'll be staying near the Vatican, about twenty minutes' walk from St Peter's Square.

We had a plan for the trip. Several plans, in fact. Yana and I have a shared list of places — what each of us wants to see. Separately there are ideas for day trips from Rome without a car, thirteen screenshots. Everything sorted, everything thought through. As will turn out later, life will rework almost all of it. And that is probably the most honest thing you can say about any plan. Man proposes, God disposes — tested more than once.

October 29 in the church calendar is the feast of the martyr Longinus the Centurion. That very Roman soldier who stood at the Cross and said: “Truly this was the Son of God.” A Roman who became a Christian at Golgotha. To my mind, a good heavenly fellow traveler for those flying to Rome.

Tomorrow evening we'll be in Rome.
